- **Hermeneutics / Hermeneutik (her-meh-noy-tik) / Hermeneutik (her-meh-noy-tik)**: Identical cognate; commonality: shared Greek root *hermēneuein* (to interpret); difference: German capitalizes nouns, Swedish does not. Ex: "Hermeneutics interprets religious texts." (Deutsch: "Hermeneutik interpretiert religiöse Texte.") / (Svenska: "Hermeneutik tolkar religiösa texter.")
- **Phenomenon / Phänomen (fay-noh-men) / Fenomen (feh-noh-men)**: Near-cognate; commonality: from Greek *phainomenon*; difference: German umlaut (ä) vs. Swedish plain e, reflecting phonetic shifts. Ex: "The phenomenon in Hindu tradition." (Deutsch: "Das Phänomen in hinduistischer Tradition.") / (Svenska: "Fenomenet i hinduistisk tradition.")
- **Tradition / Tradition (trah-dee-tsee-ohn) / Tradition (trah-dee-shoon)**: Direct cognate; commonality: Latin *traditio*; difference: Swedish softens endings, influenced by French loans. Ex: "Hinduistic tradition." (Deutsch: "Hinduistische Tradition.") / (Svenska: "Hinduistisk tradition.")
- **Text / Text (tekst) / Text (tekst)**: Identical; commonality: Proto-Germanic root; difference: German compounds freely (e.g., *Offenbarungsgeschichte*), Swedish prefers spaces or hyphens. Ex: "Revelation history as text." (Deutsch: "Offenbarungsgeschichte als Text.") / (Svenska: "Uppenbarelsehistoria som text.")
- **History / Geschichte (geh-shik-teh) / Historia (his-toh-ree-ah)**: Difference highlighted; commonality: both from Greek *historia*; German uses native *Geschichte* (related to "happen"), Swedish borrows directly. Ex: "History of revelation." (Deutsch: "Geschichte der Offenbarung.") / (Svenska: "Historia om uppenbarelsen.")
- **Religion / Religion (reh-lee-gee-ohn) / Religion (reh-lee-yoon)**: Cognate; commonality: Latin *religio*; difference: Swedish pronunciation softer, reflecting Nordic vowel harmony.

Grammar integration: German requires gender agreement (e.g., *das Phänomen* neuter), while Swedish uses common (*en*) or neuter (*ett*) without cases. Practice: "The book examines Hindu texts."
